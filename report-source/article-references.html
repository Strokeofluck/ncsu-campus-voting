Building outlines versus entrance circles

The routine-route map defaults to the original GIS footprints of both Talley and Western Boulevard, with the same outward distance buffer at each. The default is 50 meters (approximately 0.031 miles). This is an analyst-chosen comparison distance, not an official or validated visibility boundary. Display units and autoplay

Route distances are shown in miles; proximity-zone labels use meters (50 meters by default). Conversion: miles = metres ÷ 1,609.344. Calculations and raw GIS files retain metres and full precision; displayed mile values are rounded. Walking minutes = metres ÷ assumed metres/minute. The default 80 m/min is approximately 3 mph. For example, 258.4 m ÷ 1,609.344 ≈ 0.16 miles farther; 258.4 m ÷ 80 ≈ 3.2 minutes longer. A displayed proximity below 0.001 miles is labeled “<0.001 mi.”

Autoplay draws one modeled route at a time, hiding all other route lines. The default is one second per route, with a 0.25-second pause at arrival. The camera fits the full eligible route area once when playback starts, then remains fixed between routes. Point selection and the route selectors choose the trip to preview. Green routine routes enter the selected Talley zone; gray routes stay outside. These colors describe the whole route’s minimum distance to the zone, not the current animated point. The housing timing comparison now shows static route lines; it has no autoplay, moving dot or counter. Its fixed, centered labels show one-way minutes and miles. Route hover cards and Google navigation on route clicks are removed; building and site names remain available on hover. The routine map visits every eligible destination for one housing origin before moving to the next, using the current filters. The previews show no observed pedestrian traffic and do not run at real walking speed.

A hypothetical 660-space allotment at Coliseum

Using the GIS-listed totals, an allotment of 660 spaces at Coliseum Deck would account for 30.1% of its inventory and leave 1,532 spaces (69.9%) outside that hypothetical allotment.

  1. 660 ÷ 2,192 × 100 = 30.109…%, rounded to 30.1%.
  2. 2,192 − 660 = 1,532 spaces.
  3. 1,532 ÷ 2,192 × 100 = 69.890…%, rounded to 69.9%.

This is a scale comparison, not evidence that 660 spaces could operationally be reserved, that the other spaces were vacant, or that either site actually had this allotment. Existing permit commitments, occupancy, accessible-space needs and election-day operational arrangements are not established by these inventory records.

Why this is labeled GIS inventory

Other university publications use different McKimmon figures: a university job description describes 650 spaces, while a January 2025 OLLI notice describes more than 700. The sources do not establish why these figures differ. We therefore retain one reproducible GIS definition for both locations and disclose the disagreement, rather than present a verified current physical capacity. The GIS field includes different parking-use categories, including marked motorcycle allocations, and does not establish permission to park; these are not independently audited car-only capacities.

Similarly, Transportation’s visitor-parking page lists 331 spaces for Coliseum’s lower-level pay lot; that is a visitor subset, not the whole deck, and differs from the downloaded visitor-zone records. Neither that subset nor the full deck total is a confirmed voter allotment. These figures cannot establish which site had more parking available specifically for voters.
