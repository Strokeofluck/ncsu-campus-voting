Average walk to the two sites: building-by-building calculation

This is an equally weighted average across buildings, not students. The original baseline uses the same 36 buildings with accepted entrance connections to both locations. Four supplemental comparisons are available in the explorer: Aurora has a recovered mapped-entrance route and three buildings retain labeled edge estimates. They do not silently revise the original 36-building average. Each building contributes once.

  1. Add the Talley route distances for those buildings: approximately 45,736.7 metres.
  2. Divide by 36: ≈ 1,270.5 metres per building.
  3. Add the Western Boulevard distances for the same buildings: approximately 55,040.2 metres. Divide by 36: ≈ 1,528.9 metres per building.
  4. Subtract the unrounded means: Western − Talley = ≈ 258.4 metres farther. This also equals the average of the paired differences.
  5. At the default assumed 80 metres/minute: 258.4 ÷ 80 ≈ 3.2 extra minutes one way. Changing the pace changes estimated time, not distance.

Housing mix matters: residence halls average 909.7 metres farther; Wolf Village averages 1,124.2 metres closer; Wolf Ridge averages 285.9 metres closer. Each building has equal weight, regardless of beds or occupancy. Route-model limitations and unconfirmed election-door locations apply to all averages.

Calculations use full precision; displayed numbers above are rounded. Individual paired inputs · Unrounded means, sums and denominators · Routing method · Interactive average and map.

A hypothetical 660-space allotment at Coliseum

Using the GIS-listed totals, an allotment of 660 spaces at Coliseum Deck would account for 30.1% of its inventory and leave 1,532 spaces (69.9%) outside that hypothetical allotment.

  1. 660 ÷ 2,192 × 100 = 30.109…%, rounded to 30.1%.
  2. 2,192 − 660 = 1,532 spaces.
  3. 1,532 ÷ 2,192 × 100 = 69.890…%, rounded to 69.9%.

This is a scale comparison, not evidence that 660 spaces could operationally be reserved, that the other spaces were vacant, or that either site actually had this allotment. Existing permit commitments, occupancy, accessible-space needs and election-day operational arrangements are not established by these inventory records.

Why this is labeled GIS inventory

Other university publications use different McKimmon figures: a university job description describes 650 spaces, while a January 2025 OLLI notice describes more than 700. The sources do not establish why these figures differ. We therefore retain one reproducible GIS definition for both locations and disclose the disagreement, rather than present a verified current physical capacity. The GIS field includes different parking-use categories, including marked motorcycle allocations, and does not establish permission to park; these are not independently audited car-only capacities.

Similarly, Transportation’s visitor-parking page lists 331 spaces for Coliseum’s lower-level pay lot; that is a visitor subset, not the whole deck, and differs from the downloaded visitor-zone records. Neither that subset nor the full deck total is a confirmed voter allotment. These figures cannot establish which site had more parking available specifically for voters.

Building outlines versus entrance circles

The routine-route map defaults to the original GIS footprints of both Talley and Western Boulevard, with the same outward distance buffer at each. The default is 50 meters (approximately 0.031 miles). This is an analyst-chosen comparison distance, not an official or validated visibility boundary. It counts 334 of 756 modeled pairs (44.2%) near Talley and 0 of 756 near Western Boulevard, excluding trips ending at Talley from both denominators. The optional equal-radius entrance circles count 25 of 756 (3.3%) near Talley at the same radius. These measure different definitions of proximity. Neither verifies seeing the voting room or actually voting.

Why use a 50-meter zone?

We use 50 meters as an exploratory short-walk proximity measure. At the walking model’s default assumed pace of 80 meters per minute (about 3 mph), an unobstructed 50-meter walk would take less than a minute:

  1. 50 meters ÷ 80 meters per minute = 0.625 minutes.
  2. 0.625 × 60 = 37.5 seconds, or about 38 seconds.

This explains the short-walk interpretation of the chosen threshold; it is not an official GIS or election boundary or a validated measure of seeing a voting site. In the default footprint view, proximity is the minimum straight-line distance from the modeled route to the building outline. The building edge may differ from the voting entrance, and intervening barriers, street crossings and access restrictions can make the actual walk longer. Therefore, a route entering the zone is not evidence of reaching the voting door in under a minute. The optional 25- and 100-meter thresholds allow sensitivity checks; the same chosen threshold applies to both buildings.

Why four housing buildings needed separate estimates

Missing routes were failures of the entrance-connection model, not evidence that a building cannot be reached on foot. Aurora’s straight entrance connector passed through its GIS footprint; Gold and Welch had no enabled mapped entrance within the model’s 10-metre building threshold; University Towers’ candidate entrances were more than 30 metres from the connected network.

Display units and autoplay

Route distances are shown in miles; proximity-zone labels use meters (50 meters by default). Conversion: miles = metres ÷ 1,609.344. Calculations and raw GIS files retain metres and full precision; displayed mile values are rounded. Walking minutes = metres ÷ assumed metres/minute. The default 80 m/min is approximately 3 mph. For example, 258.4 m ÷ 1,609.344 ≈ 0.16 miles farther; 258.4 m ÷ 80 ≈ 3.2 minutes longer. A displayed proximity below 0.001 miles is labeled “<0.001 mi.”

Autoplay draws one modeled route at a time, hiding all other route lines. The default is one second per route, with a 0.25-second pause at arrival and a faster half-second option. The camera fits the full eligible route area once when playback starts, then remains fixed between routes. Checking Show all routes pauses autoplay and restores the full route network. Green routine routes enter the selected Talley zone; gray routes stay outside. These colors describe the whole route’s minimum distance to the zone, not the current animated point. The housing timing comparison now shows static route lines; it has no autoplay, moving dot or counter. Its walking estimates remain in the details and route hovers. The routine map visits every eligible destination for one housing origin before moving to the next, using the current filters. The previews show no observed pedestrian traffic and do not run at real walking speed.

Buildings tagged Office or Laboratory may also contain classes, and small cafes and branch libraries are not exhaustively modeled.
