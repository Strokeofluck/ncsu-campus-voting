Talley to Western Boulevard

NC State campus voting · Sources, figures and calculations

College-age voters made up a similar share of ballots at Talley in 2022 and Western Boulevard in 2026. But their ballot count grew 40.5% at the site, while it more than doubled across Wake County. Download the untouched originals and formula-linked Excel below to follow the comparison.

Quick glance

Similar age mix, different ballot growth

The complete proof package is above. Go to the originals and Excel download. The figures below are a quick reference.

__PERIOD_NOTE__

  1. The site’s age mix barely changed

    Ages 18–24: 32.1% of Talley’s ballots in 2022 and 31.8% of Western Boulevard’s in 2026.

  2. The site’s college-age count grew

    2,999 → 4,214 ballots: a 40.5% increase.

  3. Wake’s college-age count grew faster

    11,287 → 22,980 ballots: a 103.6% increase, more than double.

A nearly unchanged percentage within the site does not mean the site kept pace with college-age voting across Wake. These counts raise a question about the move; they do not establish its effect.

03 · Original sources

The government files used here

Each year comes from NCSBE’s by-county absentee archive, which includes in-person early-voting records. The Wake CSV is the input; the campus totals and percentages are calculations from those records.

Definitions, overlapping groups and calculation rules
  • One qualifying original record counts as one accepted ballot.
  • Site age share = that age group’s site ballots ÷ all site ballots × 100.
  • Count growth = (2026 count − baseline count) ÷ baseline count × 100.
  • Share change = 2026 unrounded share − baseline unrounded share, in percentage points.
  • Ages 18–22 overlap ages 18–24. Same-day-registration ballots also belong to the all-age and applicable age totals. Do not add overlapping groups.
  • 2024 was a presidential election. The primary 2022-to-2026 comparison uses two midterms.
  • Earlier years use finalized records; the 2026 file is preliminary. Matching the number of voting days does not recreate the historical reporting lag.
