Talley to Western Boulevard

NC State campus voting · Sources, figures and calculations

College-age voters made up a similar share of ballots at Talley in 2022 and Western Boulevard in 2026. But their ballot count grew 40.5% at the site, while it more than doubled across Wake County. Download the untouched originals and formula-linked Excel below to follow the comparison.

How the Excel download proves both growth figures
  1. Untouched originals: 01 Original files holds the exact government CSV bytes. SHA-256 hashes identify each source snapshot.
  2. Full Excel copies: Copy 2022, Copy 2024 and Copy 2026 retain every original column, row, header and text value in order. Their worksheet row numbers match original CSV record numbers.
  3. Highlighted source fields: 1 Source rows links the site extract to the Copy cells. 5 County source does the same for every original age-18–24 candidate, including rejected and outside-window records. Yellow headers identify the seven fields taken from the originals.
  4. Record-by-record decisions: 2 Filter flags and 6 County flags test each rule with real formulas. Green contributions add 1; excluded candidates add 0. Running totals use SUM.
  5. Count and cross-check: 3 Counts calculates site totals. 7 County counts uses SUM over every contribution, checks it with COUNTIFS, and reconciles named-site plus other-Wake ballots.
  6. Actual growth formulas: 8 Growth proof links those counts, subtracts the baseline, divides by that baseline, and multiplies by 100 in separate calculation cells. The same tab separates the nearly unchanged college-age share within the site from the site’s declining share of countywide college-age ballots. 9 Individual ages lists each age and its formula-calculated site ballot count in all three years.

Quick glance

Similar age mix, different ballot growth

The complete proof package is above. Go to the originals and Excel download. The figures below are a quick reference.

Talley: October 20, 2022–November 4, 2022. Western Boulevard: October 15, 2026–October 30, 2026. Both elections are midterms.

  1. The site’s age mix barely changed

    Ages 18–24: 32.1% of Talley’s ballots in 2022 and 31.8% of Western Boulevard’s in 2026.

  2. The site’s college-age count grew

    2,999 → 4,214 ballots: a 40.5% increase.

  3. Wake’s college-age count grew faster

    11,287 → 22,980 ballots: a 103.6% increase, more than double.

A nearly unchanged percentage within the site does not mean the site kept pace with college-age voting across Wake. These counts raise a question about the move; they do not establish its effect.

04 · Selection and definitions

What gets counted

Original fieldRule used in the comparison
county_descWAKE
ballot_req_typeONE-STOP, ONE STOP or EARLY VOTING
ballot_rtn_statusACCEPTED
ballot_rtn_dtWithin that year’s matched 16-day window, including both endpoints.
site_nameThe exact Talley or Western Boulevard label shown with each source. Countywide comparisons keep all Wake sites.
age18–24 for the college-age comparison. Age bands are inclusive; 65+ uses ages 65–120. Invalid or outside-range ages stay in the all-age total.
sdrY or YES for same-day-registration ballots, across all ages.
Definitions, overlapping groups and calculation rules
  • One qualifying original record counts as one accepted ballot.
  • Site age share = that age group’s site ballots ÷ all site ballots × 100.
  • Count growth = (2026 count − baseline count) ÷ baseline count × 100.
  • Share change = 2026 unrounded share − baseline unrounded share, in percentage points.
  • Ages 18–22 overlap ages 18–24. Same-day-registration ballots also belong to the all-age and applicable age totals. Do not add overlapping groups.
  • 2024 was a presidential election. The primary 2022-to-2026 comparison uses two midterms.
  • Earlier years use finalized records; the 2026 file is preliminary. Matching the number of voting days does not recreate the historical reporting lag.
