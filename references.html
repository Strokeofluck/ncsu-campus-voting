How we calculated the growth gap

Start with four ballot counts. Compare Talley in 2022 with Western Boulevard in 2026 and calculate growth across Wake County, then subtract the two growth rates.

Talley: October 20, 2022–November 4, 2022. Western Boulevard: October 15, 2026–October 30, 2026. First 16 days in both years; 2026 is preliminary.

Which original records qualify?
  1. county_desc = WAKE.
  2. ballot_req_type is ONE-STOP, ONE STOP, or EARLY VOTING; ballot_rtn_status = ACCEPTED.
  3. Keep only the selected age group and the matched dates above.
  4. For the Talley → Western Boulevard comparison, use TA - NCSU TALLEY STUDENT CENTER in 2022 and NCSU WESTERN BLVD BUSINESS SERVICES in 2026. For Wake County, include every site.
  5. Each qualifying ballot contributes one. The original-record audit found no duplicate accepted early-voter IDs.

2. Calculate growth from Talley to Western Boulevard

  1. 4,214 − 2,999 = 1,215 additional ballots.
  2. 1,215 ÷ 2,999 = 0.4051350….
  3. Multiply by 100 ≈ 40.5135045…%. Round to one decimal: 40.5%.

Talley → Western Boulevard growth: +40.5%

3. Calculate growth across Wake County

  1. 22,980 − 11,287 = 11,693 additional ballots.
  2. 11,693 ÷ 11,287 = 1.0359706….
  3. Multiply by 100 ≈ 103.5970586…%. Round to one decimal: 103.6%.

Wake County growth: +103.6%

4. Subtract Wake’s growth from Talley → Western Boulevard growth

40.5135045…% − 103.5970586…% ≈ -63.0835541… percentage points.

Talley → Western Boulevard growth was 63.1 percentage points behind Wake’s growth.

A negative gap means growth from Talley (2022) to Western Boulevard (2026) was slower than Wake’s; a positive gap means it was faster. This is a difference in percentage points. Calculations use full precision; final rates and gaps are rounded to one decimal.

Ages 18–24: keeping pace with Wake’s growth

This worked example is always for the youngest age group, ages 18–24. It uses the same first 16 early-voting days and source counts above.

Benchmark assumption: Western Boulevard in 2026 retains the share of Wake’s college-age early ballots that Talley served in 2022. Wake includes the named location.

  1. Wake’s ages 18–24 early ballots: 11,287 in 2022 → 22,980 in 2026.
  2. County growth multiplier: 22,980 ÷ 11,287 ≈ 2.0359706… times the 2022 count.
  3. Apply that multiplier to Talley’s 2,999 ballots: 2,999 × (22,980 ÷ 11,287) = 6,105.8757863… ≈ 6,106 ballots at the constant-share benchmark.
  4. Western Boulevard actually recorded 4,214 early ballots from ages 18–24 in 2026.
  5. Benchmark minus actual: 6,105.8757863… − 4,214 = 1,891.8757863… ≈ 1,892 ballots below the benchmark.

Ages 18–24: about 6,106 ballots at the benchmark versus 4,214 actual—about 1,892 fewer at Western Boulevard than if it had kept pace with Wake’s same-age growth.

The benchmark holds the location’s countywide share constant; it is not a prediction or a count of people who did not vote. Voters could have used another location or voting method. This calculation does not establish an effect of the relocation. Intermediate calculations retain full precision; the benchmark and difference are rounded only for the final ballot counts.

Average walk to the two sites: building-by-building calculation

This is an equally weighted average across buildings, not students. Use the same 36 buildings with usable routes to both locations; exclude the 4 unavailable buildings from both averages. Each building contributes once.

  1. Add the Talley route distances for those buildings: approximately 45,736.7 metres.
  2. Divide by 36: ≈ 1,270.5 metres per building.
  3. Add the Western Boulevard distances for the same buildings: approximately 55,040.2 metres. Divide by 36: ≈ 1,528.9 metres per building.
  4. Subtract the unrounded means: Western − Talley = ≈ 258.4 metres farther. This also equals the average of the paired differences.
  5. At the default assumed 80 metres/minute: 258.4 ÷ 80 ≈ 3.2 extra minutes one way. Changing the pace changes estimated time, not distance.

Housing mix matters: residence halls average 909.7 metres farther; Wolf Village averages 1,124.2 metres closer; Wolf Ridge averages 285.9 metres closer. Each building has equal weight, regardless of beds or occupancy. Route-model limitations and unconfirmed election-door locations apply to all averages.

Calculations use full precision; displayed numbers above are rounded. Individual paired inputs · Unrounded means, sums and denominators · Routing method · Interactive average and map.

Modeled routine walks near each site

The walk-past prototype measures the share of equally weighted modeled housing-to-teaching/library/dining routes within a chosen distance of the mapped entrance point (equal circles, default) or building footprint (original buffers). It does not estimate real student probabilities or traffic. Both site percentages use the same eligible route pairs; the highlighted lines and matching-route review list use the identical distance basis and filters; trips ending at Talley are excluded by default and can be included as sensitivity.

Exactly which destinations were modeled?

This is a curated 22-destination model—not every campus teaching, dining or library location. The inputs are 16 buildings tagged Classroom in GIS, 3 tagged Dining, Talley (tagged Student Center, added for dining using a separate university source), and 2 selected main libraries.

“Teaching” and “Dining” are model categories. GIS USE_NAME is the original university building-use field. These data do not verify class schedules, hours, student access or actual visits. Buildings tagged Office or Laboratory may also contain classes, and small cafes and branch libraries are not exhaustively modeled.
